An inference is a sensible idea supported by clues in the text. It goes one step beyond repeating what the writer says. Here is an original practice passage.

## Read the passage

> When the library doors opened, Mina was already waiting outside. She checked the folded timetable in her pocket for the third time. Inside, she chose the desk nearest the window, laid out two sharpened pencils, and took a slow breath before opening her notebook.

**Question:** What impression do you get of Mina as she prepares to study? Support your idea with a detail from the passage.

## Build an answer in three moves

1. **Make a point:** Mina seems determined to prepare carefully.
2. **Choose evidence:** She is “already waiting outside” when the library opens and has checked her timetable repeatedly.
3. **Explain the link:** Arriving early and checking her plan suggest that this study session matters to her and that she wants to use the time well.

A complete answer could be:

> Mina seems organised and serious about her work. She is waiting before the library opens and checks her timetable several times, which suggests she has planned the session and does not want to waste time.

This is stronger than saying only “Mina is organised” because the reader can see **why** that conclusion fits. It is also safer than claiming to know exactly what exam Mina has or how she will perform; the passage does not tell us those things.

## Your turn

What might the “slow breath” suggest about how Mina feels? Write one sentence with an idea, a short quotation, and an explanation.

**Possible answer:** Mina may feel nervous as well as prepared. Taking “a slow breath” before opening her notebook suggests she is calming herself before she begins. Another interpretation can work if the text supports it.
